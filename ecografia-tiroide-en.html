What it is and what it is for

The thyroid is a small gland at the base of the neck that regulates metabolism. Thyroid ultrasound lets me look at its shape, size and structure and detect any nodules, assessing their number, size and features. It is the first-choice scan for looking at the thyroid itself, because it uses no radiation and can be repeated over time.

When to have it

It is useful when your doctor finds a nodule or an abnormality, when thyroid hormone levels are abnormal, for a swelling or discomfort in the neck, or as a check over time if you already have a thyroid problem that needs following or a family history of thyroid disease.

How it works

You lie down and I place the probe on your neck with a little gel. Nothing more is needed: no needles, no radiation and usually no discomfort: if the pressure of the probe bothers you, just tell me. The scan usually takes 15–20 minutes.

Is any preparation needed?

No. Thyroid ultrasound needs no fasting and no special preparation: just avoid creams on your neck and remove necklaces and long earrings. If you have previous tests or scans, bring them with you: they help with comparison and following changes over time.

Do

  • Bring any previous reports or scans
  • Wear comfortable clothes

What I check

I assess the size and structure of the gland, the presence and features of any nodules and of the neck lymph nodes. At the end I give you the report and explain in plain words what was found and whether further checks are needed.

Frequently asked questions

Does thyroid ultrasound hurt?

No, it is painless. The probe is placed on the neck with a little gel. It uses ultrasound waves, not radiation: it is safe and can be repeated without any issue.

Is fasting or preparation needed?

No. You can eat and drink normally before the scan. Just avoid creams on your neck and remove necklaces and long earrings.

Does the ultrasound detect thyroid nodules?

Yes. I assess the presence, number, size and features of nodules, as well as the structure of the gland. If needed, I note it in the report so that your doctor or endocrinologist can decide on further tests.

Does the ultrasound replace thyroid blood tests?

No. Ultrasound shows the shape of the gland and any nodules; blood tests, such as TSH (the hormone that controls the thyroid), show how it is working. They are complementary.

If a nodule is found, what happens next?

The report describes the size and features of the nodule. Based on these, your family doctor or endocrinologist (the gland specialist) decides whether a follow-up check is enough or whether further tests are needed.

Should I remove creams or jewellery?

Yes: remove necklaces and long earrings and avoid creams on your neck on the day of the scan, so the gel adheres well to the skin.
